📖 Lesson 1: Basic Operations

Addition (+): Combining numbers to find the sum.

Subtraction (−): Taking one number away from another.

Multiplication (×): Repeated addition.

Division (÷): Splitting into equal parts.

📖 Lesson 2: Fractions

Numerator: Number above the line → parts we have.

Denominator: Number below the line → total parts.

Proper: ½   Improper: ³⁄₂   Mixed: 1½

📖 Lesson 3: Decimals & Percent

Decimal → Percent: Multiply by 100 → 0.25 = 25%

Percent → Decimal: Divide by 100 → 50% = 0.50

📖 Lesson 4: Algebraic Expressions

An algebraic expression combines numbers, variables, and operations. In 3x + 5, x is the variable, 3 is its coefficient, and 5 is a constant. Combine like terms only: 2x + 4x = 6x.

📖 Lesson 5: Linear Equations

A linear equation has a variable with exponent 1. Solve by keeping both sides balanced. Example: x + 7 = 12, so subtract 7 from both sides and x = 5. Check by substituting the answer.

📖 Lesson 6: Functions and Relations

A relation is a set of ordered pairs. A function assigns exactly one output to each input. In f(x) = 2x + 1, f(3) = 2(3) + 1 = 7.

📖 Lesson 7: Sets and Set Operations

A set is a collection of distinct objects. The union (A ∪ B) contains elements in either set; the intersection (A ∩ B) contains elements common to both. The empty set has no elements.

📖 Lesson 8: Rational Expressions

A rational expression is a ratio of polynomials, such as (x + 1)/(x − 2). The denominator cannot equal zero, so x ≠ 2 in this example. Simplify by factoring and cancelling common factors.

📖 Lesson 9: Exponents and Radicals

Exponent rules include aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ and aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ when the denominator is nonzero. A square root asks which nonnegative number multiplied by itself gives the radicand; √49 = 7.

📖 Lesson 10: Percent, Markup, and Discount

Percent means per hundred. To find 15% of ₱800, calculate 0.15 × 800 = ₱120. A discount is subtracted from the original price; a markup is added to a cost.

📖 Lesson 1: What is Matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. Everything around you is matter — air, water, books, even your body!

📖 Lesson 2: States of Matter

Solid: Definite shape & volume — e.g., rock, book

Liquid: Definite volume, takes shape — e.g., water, milk

Gas: No definite shape or volume — e.g., air, oxygen

📖 Lesson 3: Changes in Matter

Physical Change: Only appearance changes — same substance (melting ice)

Chemical Change: New substance formed (burning wood, rusting)

📖 Lesson 4: Atoms and Molecules

An atom is the basic unit of an element. Atoms contain protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons around it. A molecule forms when two or more atoms bond, such as H₂O.

📖 Lesson 5: Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures

An element contains one kind of atom. A compound has elements chemically combined in fixed proportions. A mixture combines substances physically and can often be separated by methods such as filtration.

📖 Lesson 6: Force and Motion

A force is a push or pull that can change an object's motion. Speed is distance divided by time. A balanced force does not change an object's motion; an unbalanced force causes acceleration.

📖 Lesson 7: Energy and Its Forms

Energy is the ability to do work or cause change. Common forms include kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical, and light energy. Energy can transfer or change form.

📖 Lesson 8: Heat Transfer

Conduction transfers heat through direct contact, convection transfers heat through the movement of fluids, and radiation transfers energy through waves. Heat naturally flows from warmer objects to cooler ones.

📖 Lesson 9: Ecosystems and Food Chains

An ecosystem includes living organisms and their nonliving environment. Producers make their own food, consumers eat other organisms, and decomposers break down dead matter. Arrows in a food chain show energy flow.

📖 Lesson 10: Weather and Climate

Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions, such as today's rain or temperature. Climate describes long-term patterns in a region. Temperature, air pressure, wind, humidity, and precipitation help describe weather.

📖 Lesson 1: Our Pre-Colonial Past

Before the Spaniards arrived, Filipinos already had their own government (Barangay), writing system (Baybayin), social structure, and trading with neighboring Asian countries.

📖 Lesson 2: Spanish Colonization

1521 — Arrival of Ferdinand Magellan

1565 — Spanish rule began under Miguel López de Legazpi

333 years of Spanish colonization followed.

📖 Lesson 3: The Philippine Revolution

1896 — Revolution started led by Andres Bonifacio and the Katipunan.

June 12, 1898 — Philippine Independence declared in Kawit, Cavite by Emilio Aguinaldo.

📖 Lesson 4: Pre-Colonial Filipino Communities

Before Spanish colonization, many communities were organized into barangays led by a datu. People farmed, fished, crafted goods, traded with neighboring communities, and maintained diverse customs and beliefs.

📖 Lesson 5: The Arrival of Islam in the Philippines

Islam spread to parts of the Philippines through trade and religious teachers before Spanish rule. Muslim sultanates developed especially in Mindanao and Sulu, with their own political and cultural traditions.

📖 Lesson 6: The Galleon Trade

The Manila–Acapulco Galleon Trade connected the Philippines and New Spain from 1565 to 1815. It moved goods across the Pacific and influenced Manila's role in international trade.

📖 Lesson 7: The Propaganda Movement

In the late 19th century, Filipino reformists called for changes under Spanish rule. Writers such as José Rizal, Marcelo H. del Pilar, and Graciano López Jaena used essays and publications to argue for reforms.

📖 Lesson 8: The Katipunan and Revolution

The Katipunan was a secret society founded in 1892 that sought independence from Spain. The Philippine Revolution began in 1896, with leaders including Andrés Bonifacio and other revolutionaries.

📖 Lesson 9: The First Philippine Republic

On June 12, 1898, Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence. The Malolos Constitution was adopted and the First Philippine Republic was inaugurated in 1899, amid conflict with the United States.

📖 Lesson 10: Philippines During World War II

Japan occupied the Philippines during World War II from 1942 until Allied and Filipino forces regained control in 1944–1945. The war caused widespread destruction and hardship for civilians.

📖 Lesson 1: The Communication Process

Sender → Message → Channel → Receiver → Feedback

Communication is the process of sharing information, ideas, and feelings between two or more people.

📖 Lesson 2: Verbal & Non-Verbal Communication

Verbal: Using words — speaking, writing

Non-Verbal: Without words — facial expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact

📖 Lesson 3: Barriers to Communication

Things that prevent clear communication:

• Noise • Language differences • Cultural differences • Distractions

📖 Lesson 4: Purpose and Audience

Effective communication begins by identifying the purpose and audience. A message may inform, persuade, entertain, or express feelings. Choose details, tone, and vocabulary that suit the listeners or readers.

📖 Lesson 5: Active Listening

Active listening means giving attention, avoiding interruptions, asking clarifying questions, and paraphrasing key points. It helps reduce misunderstandings and shows respect for the speaker.

📖 Lesson 6: Written Communication

Written communication includes letters, emails, reports, and messages. Organize ideas clearly, use appropriate language, check grammar and spelling, and consider the reader's needs.

📖 Lesson 7: Visual Communication

Visual communication uses images, charts, diagrams, symbols, and layout to explain information. Keep visuals readable, label data clearly, and use visuals that support rather than distract from the message.

📖 Lesson 8: Public Speaking Basics

A clear speech usually has an introduction, organized main points, and a conclusion. Practice aloud, speak at a steady pace, use appropriate volume, and make eye contact when suitable.

📖 Lesson 9: Giving and Receiving Feedback

Helpful feedback is specific, respectful, and focused on the message or behavior rather than personal attacks. When receiving feedback, listen carefully, ask questions, and consider useful suggestions.

📖 Lesson 10: Communication in Digital Spaces

Digital communication includes chat, email, video calls, and online posts. Use respectful language, protect private information, check facts before sharing, and consider how a message may be interpreted without facial cues.

📖 Lesson 1: Introduction to Programming

Programming is the process of writing instructions that a computer can execute to perform a task or solve a problem.

Program: A set of instructions written in a programming language.

Algorithm: A step-by-step procedure for solving a problem.

Common languages: Python, Java, JavaScript, and C++.

Good practice: Understand the problem, plan the steps, write the code, test it, and fix errors.

📖 Lesson 2: Variables and Data Types

A variable is a named place used to store a value that a program can use.

Integer: Whole numbers, such as 12.

Float/Decimal: Numbers with a decimal point, such as 3.14.

String: Text, such as "Hello".

Boolean: A true-or-false value.

Example in Python: student_name = "Ana" and age = 16.

📖 Lesson 3: Input, Output, and Operators

Input receives information from the user. Output displays information to the user.

In Python, input() receives text and print() displays output.

Arithmetic operators: + addition, - subtraction, * multiplication, / division, and % remainder.

Example: print(5 + 3) displays 8.

📖 Lesson 4: Algorithms and Flowcharts

An algorithm is a sequence of steps for solving a problem. A flowchart represents those steps with symbols: ovals for start/end, rectangles for processes, diamonds for decisions, and arrows for flow.

📖 Lesson 5: Conditional Statements

A conditional statement runs code depending on whether a condition is true. In Python, if, elif, and else select which block runs. Indentation determines which statements belong to each block.

📖 Lesson 6: Loops and Repetition

Loops repeat instructions. A for loop is useful for iterating through a sequence or range; a while loop repeats while its condition is true. Make sure a while loop can eventually stop.

📖 Lesson 7: Functions

A function is a reusable block of code that performs a task. Functions can accept parameters and return values. In Python, define one with def, followed by its name and parentheses.

📖 Lesson 8: Lists and Collections

A list stores multiple items in one ordered collection. Python lists use square brackets, such as scores = [85, 90, 78]. Indexing starts at 0, so scores[0] is 85.

📖 Lesson 9: Debugging and Testing

A syntax error breaks language rules; a runtime error happens while a program runs; a logic error produces an incorrect result. Test normal, boundary, and unusual inputs, then fix and retest.

📖 Lesson 10: Introduction to HTML

HTML structures content on web pages using elements called tags. Headings use h1–h6, paragraphs use p, links use a, and images use img. Many elements have opening and closing tags.

📖 Aralin 1: Kahulugan at Kahalagahan ng Komunikasyon

Ang komunikasyon ay proseso ng pagpapadala at pagtanggap ng mensahe, ideya, impormasyon, at damdamin sa pagitan ng mga tao.

Kahalagahan: Nakakatulong ito sa pag-unawa sa isa't isa, pagbabahagi ng kaalaman, paglutas ng suliranin, at pagpapatibay ng ugnayan.

Mabisang komunikasyon ang malinaw, angkop, magalang, at nauunawaan ng kausap.

📖 Aralin 2: Mga Elemento ng Komunikasyon

Tagapagpadala: Taong bumubuo at nagpapadala ng mensahe.

Mensahe: Impormasyong nais ipahayag.

Daluyan: Paraan ng pagpapadala, tulad ng pagsasalita, sulat, o online message.

Tagatanggap: Taong tumatanggap at nagbibigay-kahulugan sa mensahe.

Feedback: Tugon ng tagatanggap na nagpapakita kung naunawaan ang mensahe.

Ingay o hadlang: Anumang nakahahadlang sa malinaw na pag-unawa.

📖 Aralin 3: Wasto at Angkop na Paggamit ng Wika

Gamitin ang wikang angkop sa kausap, layunin, at sitwasyon.

✅ Maging malinaw at tiyak sa pagpili ng mga salita.

✅ Igalang ang pananaw at damdamin ng kausap.

✅ Iwasan ang malabong pahayag at hindi kailangang pananalita.

✅ Makinig nang mabuti at magtanong kung may hindi nauunawaan.

📖 Lesson 4: Berbal at Di-Berbal na Komunikasyon

Ang berbal na komunikasyon ay gumagamit ng mga salita, pasalita man o pasulat. Ang di-berbal ay gumagamit ng ekspresyon ng mukha, kilos, tindig, kumpas, at tono upang magpahiwatig ng kahulugan.

📖 Lesson 5: Antas at Barayti ng Wika

Nag-iiba ang paggamit ng wika ayon sa sitwasyon, lugar, pangkat, at layunin. Piliin ang angkop na salita at antas ng pormalidad batay sa kausap at konteksto.

📖 Lesson 6: Pagbuo ng Malinaw na Mensahe

Ayusin ang ideya sa lohikal na pagkakasunod-sunod. Gumamit ng tiyak na salita, maiikling pangungusap kung kailangan, at mga pang-ugnay upang madaling masundan ang mensahe.

📖 Lesson 7: Pakikinig at Pag-unawa

Ang mahusay na tagapakinig ay nakatuon sa nagsasalita, iniiwasan ang panghuhusga habang nakikinig, at nagtatanong kung may hindi malinaw. Nakakatulong ang pagbubuod upang matiyak ang pag-unawa.

📖 Lesson 8: Komunikasyon sa Akademikong Gawain

Sa talakayan, ulat, at presentasyon, gumamit ng malinaw na estruktura at mapagkakatiwalaang impormasyon. Kilalanin ang pinagmulan ng ideya at iwasan ang pangongopya.

📖 Lesson 9: Pagsulat ng Pormal na Liham at Email

Ang pormal na liham o email ay may malinaw na paksa, magalang na pagbati, maayos na katawan, at angkop na pangwakas. Suriin ang pangalan, detalye, tono, at baybay bago ipadala.

📖 Lesson 10: Pagharap sa Hindi Pagkakaunawaan

Kapag may hindi pagkakaunawaan, manatiling kalmado, pakinggan ang magkabilang panig, linawin ang intensiyon, at gumamit ng mga pahayag na nakatuon sa sariling damdamin at pangangailangan.
